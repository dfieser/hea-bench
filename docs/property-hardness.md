## Data

Source: Borg et al. 2020 (Sci. Data 7, 430; CC-BY-4.0 figshare deposit mirrored in `data/raw/borg2020/`). 417 unique (formula, processing) alloys carry a near-room-temperature Vickers hardness after the loader rules (median over repeat measurements, test temperature at most 35 C); 384 of them are fully descriptor-scorable and form the training population, spanning 117 alloy families and HV 109 to 1183.

## Model and held-out error

Random forest (300 trees, seed 0) on the package's 14 matrix descriptors, evaluated by family-grouped five-fold cross validation (whole alloy families held out, the extrapolative protocol), with a family-grouped 20 percent calibration split inside each training fold for the conformal interval.

| quantity | value |
|---|---:|
| grouped CV mean absolute error | 115 HV (fold-to-fold standard error 13) |
| grouped CV median absolute error | 79 HV |
| conformal interval empirical coverage (nominal 90%) | 0.909 |
| mean interval width at alpha 0.1 | 658 HV |

## What this error does and does not support

A mean absolute error near 115 HV against a range of roughly 1100 HV supports coarse screening: separating soft solid-solution regimes from hard ones, triaging a palette before synthesis. It does not support ranking candidates whose predicted hardness differs by less than roughly the interval width above, and the API's intervals are wide exactly so that such pairs visibly overlap. The training data pools processing routes; conditioning on one route is available and shrinks the population (the floor refuses below 50 alloys).

## Deliberately not shipped

Yield strength: the same deposit carries 1,067 strength rows, but they span test temperatures from ambient to 1000 C with uneven strain conditions; a pooled fit would predict an ill-defined quantity, and the near-room-temperature subset thins per system to where family-grouped validation is not meaningful. Elastic moduli and experimental ductility: too few rows after the same filters. Corrosion and oxidation measures: no adequately licensed public dataset at screening scale. These return as tier B properties if and when a licensed dataset supports a defensible held-out error; the omission is the honest outcome, not a roadmap gap.
